## What Is Blue Arbor?

Blue Arbor is a fictional SaaS company with around 2,500 employees and 15,000 users. Blue Arbor provides membership-based businesses with tools to manage subscription billing, customer accounts, analytics, and support. Blue Arbor mainly works with gyms, online content creators, and other companies that offer memberships. It is located in California and provides support to users across America, along with businesses in the EU. Among its many services, Blue Arbor (BA) also offers software support for processing payments. BA uses NIST CSF 2.0 to organize its cyber-risk program, and it must also comply with GDPR, PCI DSS, and CCPA.

## Services

Subscription billing: Processes recurring payments, renewals, refunds, and invoices. It manages card data, payment tokens, billing addresses, and transaction history. This includes personal data covered by GDPR and CCPA and card data covered by PCI DSS.

Member portal: Allows users to manage their accounts, subscriptions, and privacy preferences. The portal holds personal information, including names, email addresses, password hashes, and MFA information.

Merchant dashboard: Allows businesses to manage customers, transactions, permissions, and reports.

Customer analytics: Shows retention, purchases, engagement, and trends. This creates data-transparency questions under GDPR and sale, sharing, and opt-out questions under CCPA.

Marketing automation: Manages renewal reminders, offers, and checkout messages.

Support and refund portal: Handles account problems, refunds, disputes, and attachments.

Integration marketplace: Connects the platform to payment processors, CRMs, email providers, and analytics vendors. It shares account information, transaction information, tokens, and API activity with third-party analytics and payment vendors, creating third-party risk.

## Processing Purpose Register and Data Subjects

Blue Arbor (BA) processes subscriber data by collecting names, email addresses, account IDs, and preferences to create and administer subscriber accounts and deliver purchased services. BA processes payment account holder data by using billing contact information, card tokens, transaction records, and card brands to authorize recurring payments, issue refunds, and manage renewals. BA processes customer administrator data by using business contact information, roles, permissions, and administrator activity to allow authorized representatives to manage their organizations’ BA accounts. BA processes support requester data because when a data subject contacts the help desk, they must provide account or contact information so that a ticket can be created and the data subject can be authenticated. BA processes website visitor data, including browser data, security events, and IP addresses, to operate the website and prevent abuse or malicious activity. BA processes employee names, business email addresses, employer information, and inquiry details to administer employment, meet legal requirements, and secure company systems.
